What this is

A combat model for THE FINALS. It replays a duel between two loadouts on a 10 ms tick, rolling accuracy and headshots shot by shot, applying damage dropoff by range, and forcing reloads when a magazine runs dry. Nothing is averaged ahead of time — the win rates you see come from actually running the fight.

Which data version you are running

The Data picker in the header sets the weapon numbers every screen uses — stats table, 1v1 simulation and meta analysis alike. It defaults to the newest sheet available and remembers your choice. The same picker sits in the Simulation and Meta Simulation sidebars.

Versions are ordered by their version number, not by the order the sheets were added, so a newly ingested sheet slots into the right place automatically.

Blank cells stay blank
If a sheet records a weapon but leaves a stat empty, that is the author saying "not applicable" — it is never back-filled from an older sheet.
Missing weapons inherit
If a sheet lists a weapon with no stats at all (11.3.0 does this for melee), it runs on the last version that did record it, and says so.
Reach is engine knowledge
No sheet records melee swing range or the flamethrower's cone, so those fill in from a fixed table — only where the sheet is silent.

Solved, not sampled

Meta Simulation does not run duels and count winners. Because nobody moves and nobody reacts to being shot, each fighter's shot timing is fixed and only the hit rolls are random — so the exact chance of every outcome can be worked out directly. The win rates carry no sampling error and are identical every run.

The 1v1 screen still plays duels out shot by shot, because that is what produces the animation and the combat log. Any matchup where fighters move is sampled rather than solved, from a seed, so a surprising result can be replayed exactly.

Solving also removed a bias: rounding every event to a 10 ms tick delayed shots slightly, worst on burst weapons whose burst delay does not divide into the tick. The 93R's average time to kill was about 3 ms too slow because of it.

How a tick resolves

  1. Melee fighters (or both, if melee mode is on) close distance at their class speed. In Meta Simulation nobody moves at all.
  2. Any finished reload is applied.
  3. Each player whose next-shot timer has come due fires: a roll against accuracy decides hit or miss, then a roll against headshot chance decides which damage number is used.
  4. Damage is scaled by the dropoff curve for the current range.
  5. Both players' damage is applied simultaneously — which is why true ties are possible.
  6. If neither fighter dies inside 60 seconds the duel is reported as a timeout rather than being awarded to whoever has more health left.

Stats glossary

TTK
Time to kill — seconds from the first shot to the killing blow, reloads included.
Ideal TTK
TTK at 100% accuracy, body shots only, no dropoff. A floor, not an expectation.
Dropoff
Damage is full until the min range, then falls linearly to the stated reduction at the max range.
Aim profile
An opponent skill preset used by Meta Simulation: Poor 50/20, Average 75/35, Strong 90/55, Elite 99/80 (accuracy / headshot %).
Favorable / Even / Unfavorable
Win rate ≥ 60%, 40–60%, and < 40% respectively.

Known limits

  • Healing is modelled — the four heal sources, stacked, versioned by season. Other gadgets, specializations and shields are not.
  • No cover, movement mechanics, recoil or spread growth; accuracy is a flat per-shot probability.
  • Projectile travel time is cosmetic; hits resolve on the tick they are fired.
  • Quick-reload behaviour (e.g. the ARN-220) is not implemented yet.
  • Weapon data is a community data sheet and can lag behind live patches.
  • History covers seven sheets, not every patch, so a change is attributed to a range of versions rather than to the patch that made it. Nothing is cross-checked against official patch notes yet.

DPS is sustained body-shot damage including reloads. Ideal TTK assumes every shot hits the body at point-blank range. Weapons with no RPM in the dataset — most melee — are modelled at 60 RPM, so treat their timings as placeholders. See for what is and isn't modelled.

Healing

The four healing sources, at the selected data version. Attach them to either fighter on the Simulation screen, or rank every combination of them on the Sustain screen.

Rates are averaged across a 7 second hold, which is what a cashout steal takes — a beam sustains 46 HP/s but overheats after 5.5s, so its average over a window is lower than its peak. Healing that lands on someone already at full health is lost, so a fighter never absorbs the whole figure.

Shields

The two shields the simulation models, at the selected data version. A shield is not extra health: it absorbs before health does, takes no healing, keeps none of the overflow from the shot that breaks it, and — for the Dome — expires on a timer whether or not anything hit it. Rank them alongside the healing on the screen.

The rest of the kit

Every specialization, gadget and carriable in the game, with the class that can carry it and the slot it takes. Only the shields and the healing above carry numbers; the rest are here because the loadout rules need to know what competes for which slot — that is what decides whether a squad of three can actually field a given combination of healing and shields.

Simulating any of the others needs its own model, not just its numbers: damage, radius, arm and fuse time, and what the engine is supposed to do when it goes off. Adding one to csv/gadgets/ is a row; making it mean something is a feature.

Sustain Analysis

You are on the objective and not shooting back. Which healing keeps you standing long enough to finish?

Stealing a cashout takes 7s in qualifying rounds and 6s in the final. A vault takes 20s.

A full enemy squad is three. All three are solved every run, so this switches instantly — and the ranking table shows 1v1, 2v1 and 3v1 side by side whatever is picked here.

The Mesh Shield and the Dome Shield join the kit space, which roughly triples the grid. Every combination of healing and shields a squad of three can legally carry is solved; the ones no squad can field are left out. Changing this needs another run.

The Mesh is a panel covering one arc, so whether it is between you and the shot depends on where they are standing. That is an assumption, not a measurement — it blends two already-solved kits, so it applies instantly without another run. The Dome needs no setting: whether it covers you is decided by its 4m radius against the range.

Everyone opening at once fires in perfect lockstep all fight, which roughly triples the chance of dying in the first half second. Spread is the neutral arrangement; together is the worst case. Changing this needs another run.

Run Settings

Every scenario is solved exactly — no sampling, no noise. Each cell is one kill-time walk rather than a duel, because nobody is shooting back. Extra attackers merge into that same walk, so a 3v1 costs a longer shot list and nothing else. Everyone in the squad carries the weapon named on the row.
